What the harness caught

The captured target was the Equity Research Copilot public demo — a keyless deployment whose captured responses covered two companies (NVDA, MSFT) with 10-K and transcript excerpts. The eval suite was authored against a six-company universe (adding AAPL, JPM, XOM, TSLA) with document ids like nvda_2025_10k.

The harness surfaced two distinct problems, case by case. First, the coverage gap: requests for AAPL, JPM, XOM and TSLA returned errors (error_rate 0.60–0.80 across categories), and citation rules never match because the captured document ids differed from the suite's expected labels — hence citation precision 0.00 against 1.00 on the mock.

Correction to the original interpretation: of five refusal cases, two returned nonempty responses without a detected refusal; the other three returned HTTP 404 errors with empty answers. The original scorer mislabeled those execution failures as severe hallucinations. Across the full run, five of seven original severe flags accompanied HTTP errors. Those errors cannot establish model hallucination or successful refusal.

Scorer v2 reports refusal correctness only for the $live_evaluated usable responses; $live_unavailable error/empty responses remain failed cases and are excluded from that metric's denominator. On answerable questions, “refusal correct” means the response did not refuse. It does not show that the system correctly refused unknown-company questions. The remaining severe flags are deterministic missing-refusal flags, not independently verified fabricated claims.

The original 13 July artifacts remain available unchanged in the historical archive. The methodology and rebuild instructions explain the scoring correction. Corpus coverage, source-id alignment, and relevance of the returned passages still need separate review.
